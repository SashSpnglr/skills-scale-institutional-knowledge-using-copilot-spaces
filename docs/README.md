# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

Welcome to OctoAcme's project management documentation hub. This collection of guides provides a comprehensive framework for delivering customer-focused, high-quality software through iterative, data-informed processes. Whether you're a new team member looking to understand how we work or an experienced contributor seeking process details, these docs serve as your single source of truth for project execution at OctoAcme.

## Overview

OctoAcme's project management approach follows a structured lifecycle that emphasizes customer value, iterative delivery, and clear ownership. Every project moves through five key phases: **Initiation** (validating the business need and aligning stakeholders), **Planning** (breaking work into actionable increments and defining success criteria), **Execution and Tracking** (building, testing, and monitoring progress), **Release and Deployment** (safely shipping to production with verification), and **Retrospective** (capturing learnings and driving continuous improvement). This lifecycle is supported by well-defined artifacts including Project Charters, Release Plans, Risk Registers, and Definition of Done criteria that keep teams aligned and accountable.

Cross-functional collaboration is central to our process, with distinct roles ensuring both delivery excellence and business outcomes. **Project Managers** coordinate schedules, manage risks, and maintain transparency across stakeholders. **Product Managers** define what to build, prioritize the roadmap, and measure impact through customer and business metrics. **Developers** implement features, maintain code quality, and participate in design decisions. **QA/Testing** validates acceptance criteria and quality standards. **Stakeholders** provide strategic input and approvals. These roles work together through regular communication cadences—weekly PM-PdM syncs, twice-weekly standups for delivery teams, and monthly stakeholder updates—all designed to maintain alignment while minimizing overhead.

Quality assurance practices ensure consistent delivery standards. Our Definition of Done requires acceptance criteria validation, passing unit and integration tests, code review and merge, clean security scans, and updated documentation. Continuous Integration pipelines automate testing and linting before any code reaches production. Risk management follows a structured approach: we maintain a Risk Register capturing impact, likelihood, and mitigation strategies, reviewed weekly during team syncs. Communication flows through clear escalation paths—from team-level triage to PM coordination to sponsor-level decisions—ensuring blockers are resolved quickly. Project boards serve as the single source of truth for work status, and release notes provide transparency to all stakeholders about what's changing and why.
